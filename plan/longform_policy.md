# Long-form profanity rule — sources (checked 2026-10-22)

Owner decision (2026-10-22): long-form explainers may use **one** profane word, **only in the first 7 seconds**,
only when it sharpens the hook. The Shorts stay clean. The full rule is in LONGFORM.md §6.

What the sources say:
- July 2025 TeamYouTube update (covered by India Today Technology, 2025-07-30; PPC Land, 2025-07-29): strong
  profanity such as the f-word in the **first 7 seconds** no longer triggers limited ads by itself. Strong profanity
  used with **high frequency** throughout a video, or profanity in the **title or thumbnail**, can still limit or remove ads.
  - https://www.indiatoday.in/technology/news/story/youtube-loosens-rules-on-profanity-says-swearing-in-first-7-seconds-now-okay-for-full-ads-2763586-2025-07-30
  - https://ppc.land/youtube-removes-strong-profanity-restrictions-from-first-7-seconds/
- YouTube Help, advertiser-friendly guidelines, "Inappropriate language" (read 2026-10-22):
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278
  - Full ads: obscured profanity, or "hell"/"damn" in the title, thumbnail or video. Moderate profanity
    ("shit") used frequently *in the video*.
  - Limited ads: moderate profanity in the title or thumbnail; "focal usage of profanity throughout a video
    (such as profanity used in most sentences)". The page says placement "at the start or throughout the majority
    of the video" matters.
  - No ads: stronger profanity (f-word) in titles or thumbnails; extreme profanity, hateful language or slurs anywhere.
  - On 2026-10-22 the page did **not** spell out a 7-second allowance. So before using a *strong* word, re-check
    this page. If it isn't clearly allowed, use a mild word or none.
