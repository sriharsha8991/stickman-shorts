**YouTube Shorts title:** Should hot food cool before the fridge?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Your food is hot. Should it cool before the fridge? The US food safety agency (USDA FSIS) says hot food can go straight into the refrigerator. What matters is time: between 4 °C and 60 °C (40–140 °F), bacteria can double in as little as 20 minutes, so leftovers should be in the fridge within 2 hours of cooking (within 1 hour on a day above 32 °C / 90 °F). Waiting for food to cool on the counter only adds time in that warm range. A big pot cools slowly in the middle, so split large amounts into small, shallow containers (or cool the pot in a cold-water bath) before they go in. Other food agencies say the same thing in a different way: cool and refrigerate within 2 hours. Education, not medical advice. 🍲❄️
Sources: USDA FSIS, "Leftovers and Food Safety" · USDA FSIS, "How Temperatures Affect Food" · NSW Food Authority, "Leftovers".
