**YouTube Shorts title:** Does washing raw chicken make it safer?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Raw chicken? Before you wash it, is it safer? In a 2019 US Department of Agriculture (USDA) kitchen study with home cooks, 60% of the people who washed or rinsed raw chicken had bacteria in their sink afterwards, and 14% still had bacteria there after they tried to clean the sink. The USDA says washing, rinsing or soaking meat and poultry in salt water, vinegar or lemon juice does not destroy bacteria; it only spreads them around the kitchen. Cooking kills them: chicken is safe at 74 °C (165 °F) inside, checked with a food thermometer. Hands matter too: in the same study, people who did NOT wash their chicken still moved bacteria onto salad, mostly from unwashed hands and dirty sinks and tools. So wash your hands, boards and sink, not the chicken. Education, not medical advice. 🍗🍋
Sources: USDA press release "USDA Study: Don't Wash Your Poultry…" (20 Aug 2019, usda.gov) · USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, "Washing Food: Does it Promote Food Safety?" (fsis.usda.gov).
