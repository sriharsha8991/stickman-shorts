**YouTube Shorts title:** Do blue-light glasses really protect your eyes?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Blue-light glasses? Before you buy, do they work? In 2023, a Cochrane review pooled 17 trials from six countries that compared blue-light filtering lenses with normal lenses. For tired eyes from computer use, the filtering lenses probably made no difference, and there was no evidence that they protect the back of the eye. Daylight gives your eyes far more blue light than any screen (about a thousand times more). The American Academy of Ophthalmology says there is no scientific evidence that blue light from devices damages your eyes, and it does not recommend blue-light glasses for computer use. Screens tire your eyes mostly because you blink less: try the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds). The trials were small and short, so long-term effects are not known. Education, not medical advice. 👓
Sources: Singh et al., "Blue-light filtering spectacle lenses for visual performance, sleep, and macular health in adults", Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023 (CD013244.pub2) · American Academy of Ophthalmology, "Should you be worried about blue light?".
