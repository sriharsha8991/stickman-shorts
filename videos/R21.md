**YouTube Shorts title:** Do you really need 10,000 steps a day?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Ten thousand steps? Before you walk, do you need them? The 10,000 goal started as a marketing campaign in Japan, not as a medical finding (it may trace back to a 1965 step counter called manpo-kei, "10,000 steps meter"). In 2022, researchers pooled 15 studies of 47,471 adults (Lancet Public Health). The more people walked, the lower their risk of dying during the studies, but the gains levelled off: at about 6,000–8,000 steps a day for people aged 60 and over, and about 8,000–10,000 for people under 60. A 2019 Harvard study of 16,741 older women found the same shape: 4,400 steps a day was linked to a 41% lower risk than 2,700, and the benefit levelled off around 7,500. These studies show links, not cause and effect, and more walking is still good for you. Ten thousand is simply not a magic line. Education, not medical advice. 👟
Sources: Paluch et al., "Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts", Lancet Public Health 2022 · Lee et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2019 (via Harvard Gazette, June 2019).
