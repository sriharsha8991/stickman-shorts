**YouTube Shorts title:** Is food on the floor safe for five seconds?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Dropped food? Before you eat it, is five seconds safe? Food scientists at Rutgers University tested the "five-second rule" 2,560 times: four foods (watermelon, bread, buttered bread, gummy sweets) on four surfaces (steel, tile, wood, carpet) for under 1 second, 5, 30 and 300 seconds. Germs moved onto food in under one second. Wet watermelon picked up the most, gummy sweets the least, and carpet passed far fewer germs than tile or steel. Longer contact did mean more germs, but the researchers call the five-second rule "a significant oversimplification". It was one lab test with one bacterium (a stand-in for Salmonella) on clean lab surfaces; real floors vary, and fewer germs is not the same as safe. Education, not medical advice. 🍉🍞
Sources: Miranda & Schaffner (2016), "Longer Contact Times Increase Cross-Contamination of Enterobacter aerogenes from Surfaces to Food", Applied and Environmental Microbiology, doi:10.1128/AEM.01838-16 · Rutgers University news release via ScienceDaily (9 Sep 2016).
