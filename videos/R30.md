**YouTube Shorts title:** Are frozen vegetables less healthy than fresh?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Are frozen vegetables less healthy than fresh? Myth. A two-year study (Li, Pegg et al., 2017) measured vitamin C, provitamin A and folate in eight fruits and vegetables bought fresh, frozen, or fresh and then kept five days in the fridge. Most comparisons showed no real difference, and when there was one, frozen beat the fridge-stored "fresh" more often than the other way round. Fresh produce starts losing vitamin C from the moment it is picked. Freezing is not free: blanching costs some vitamin C, and frozen bags lose a little more over many months, so just-picked fresh is best of all. Check frozen bags for added salt or sauces, and don't overboil either kind. Education, not medical advice. 🥦
Sources: Li L, Pegg RB et al., "Selected nutrient analyses of fresh, fresh-stored, and frozen fruits and vegetables", Journal of Food Composition and Analysis (2017), doi:10.1016/j.jfca.2017.02.002 · Rickman, Barrett & Bruhn, J Sci Food Agric 87:930–944 (2007, UC Davis review) · Favell, Food Chemistry 62:59–64 (1998).
