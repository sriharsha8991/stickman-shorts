**YouTube Shorts title:** Do you really lose most heat through your head?

**Description / Reels caption:**
Before grabbing a hat, does your head lose most heat? Your head is only about 7% of your body's surface. In a 2006 lab test, scientists measured heat loss with heads in and out of cold water: the head gave only about one tenth of the heat. The old "40 to 45 percent" idea came from an army test where people wore arctic suits but no hats, so the head was the only bare part. A hat still helps on a cold day, because any bare skin loses heat. 🧢
Education, not medical advice.
Sources: Pretorius, Bristow, Steinman & Giesbrecht (2006), Journal of Applied Physiology 101:669-675, "Thermal effects of whole head submersion in cold water on nonshivering humans" · Vreeman & Carroll (2008), BMJ 337:a2769, "Festive medical myths".
