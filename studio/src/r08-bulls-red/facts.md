| "Do bulls really hate the colour red?" | The belief on trial (hook) | Common belief (bullfighting cape). |
| "Cattle have only two kinds of colour cells, not three." | Confirmed | Jacobs, Deegan & Neitz 1998, "Photopigment basis for dichromatic color vision in cows, goats, and sheep", Visual Neuroscience 15(3):581-584 (abstract read on cambridge.org): two cone types, an S cone (peak ~444-455 nm) and an M/L cone (~552-555 nm). People with normal colour vision have three. |
| "So red does not stand out to a bull." | Confirmed (plain-words summary) | Follows from the same paper: with no separate long-wave (red) cone, cattle are dichromats like red-green colour-blind people, so red is not a vivid, separate colour for them. |
| "In a TV test, a bull ignored a still red flag." | Confirmed (wording note) | MythBusters, "Red Rag to a Bull" (Discovery, 2007, season 5): when a blue flag was moved and the red flag stayed still, the bull charged the blue flag (Teyit fact-check summary). In the same episode a still crew member dressed all in red was ignored while the bull chased two moving cowboys (Mental Floss, MythResults). "Ignored" fits both; one caveat: in the first test, with all flags still, the bull charged red, white and blue flags equally. |
| "It chased a moving blue flag instead. So, a myth!" | Confirmed | Same sources: the Build Team's remote-controlled clothesline showed bulls respond to movement, not colour; myth rated BUSTED. |
| "The bull charges at movement, not at colour." | Confirmed | Same; consistent with the dichromacy finding. Caveat for the description: a TV test is not a lab study; the colour-vision paper is the real evidence. |
