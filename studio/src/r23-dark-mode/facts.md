# R23 · Does dark mode really save your phone's battery? — facts (verified 2026-11-15)

Verdict: **HALF TRUE** — dark mode saves a lot of power only at high screen brightness, and only on OLED screens. At the
brightness most people use indoors, the saving is small.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on the day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Purdue engineers tested four phones. | TRUE | Purdue University news release, 28 Jul 2021, "Dark mode may not save your phone's battery life as much as you think, but there are a few silver linings" (https://www.purdue.edu/newsroom/releases/2021/Q3/dark-mode-may-not-save-your-phones-battery-life-as-much-as-you-think,-but-there-are-a-few-silver-linings.html): Charlie Hu and Pranab Dash measured six of the most-downloaded Google Play apps on the Pixel 2, Moto Z3, Pixel 4 and Pixel 5; presented at ACM MobiSys 2021 (Dash & Hu, "How much battery does dark mode save? An accurate OLED display power profiler for modern smartphones"). |
| 3 | At full brightness, it saved about forty percent. | TRUE | Same release: at 100% brightness, switching from light to dark mode "saves an average of 39%-47% battery power". |
| 4 | Indoors, it saved just three to nine percent. | TRUE | Same release: at 30%-50% brightness dark mode saves "only 3%-9% power on average"; auto-brightness "tends to keep brightness levels around 30%-40% most of the time when indoors". |
| 5 | Battery saver? Only half true! | TRUE | Lines 3-4: a big saving only at high brightness, a small one at normal indoor brightness. |
| 6 | It only works on screens whose black pixels switch off. | TRUE | Same release: OLED screens have no backlight like the LCD screens of older phones, so dark pixels draw less power; on an LCD the backlight stays on whatever the colour. On-screen tag: "OLED ONLY". |

Caveats said in plain words / in the description:
- The press release says "battery power"; the study measured the power drawn by the screen in six popular apps, so the
  real gain in hours depends on how much of your battery the screen uses.
- Four Android phones (2017-2020 models) and six apps; newer phones were not in the test.
- "Indoors" = the 30-50% brightness that auto-brightness usually picks indoors (per the release).
