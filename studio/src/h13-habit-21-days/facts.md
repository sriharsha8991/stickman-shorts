# H13 · 21 days to form a habit is a myth — facts

Re-verified 2026-10-22.

| Claim (script / screen) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The "21 days" figure traces to Maxwell Maltz's 1960 book Psycho-Cybernetics: "It usually requires a minimum of about 21 days to effect any perceptible change in a mental image", from patients adjusting after plastic surgery (new face), phantom limbs etc. — not habit formation | Confirmed | UCL Health Behaviour Research Centre blog (Lally & Gardner team), 2012: https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/bsh/2012/06/29 |
| Researchers tracked habits daily for 12 weeks (84 days) | Confirmed | Lally P, van Jaarsveld CHM, Potts HWW, Wardle J. "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world." Eur J Soc Psychol 2010;40(6):998–1009, doi:10.1002/ejsp.674 |
| 96 volunteers recruited (82 analysed; 39 with a good model fit) | Confirmed | Lally 2010 via https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit and https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=7463 |
| Median 66 days to reach automaticity (95% of plateau) | Confirmed (median among participants with a good fit) | same |
| Range 18 to 254 days; 254 is projected beyond the 84-day study | Confirmed; "254 = projected" on screen | same |
| Simple behaviours (a glass of water) became automatic faster than complex ones (50 sit-ups) | Confirmed | UCL BSH blog 2012 |
| Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation | Confirmed | Lally 2010 (as reported by the sources above) |
| Caveat: small study, simple habits | Kept on screen + in script | — |
| Later review agrees: median 59–66 days, individual range 4–335 days (not used on screen) | Context | Singh B et al. 2024, Healthcare 12(23):2488 https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/fe6ba93e-1d6b-42b8-9ee7-a231a64ff006 |
| "Missed a day? Just show up the next." | Advice framing consistent with the one-missed-day finding; no claim about missing two days | — |
