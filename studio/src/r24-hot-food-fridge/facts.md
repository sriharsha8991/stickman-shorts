# R24 · Should hot food cool before the fridge? — facts (verified 15 Nov 2026)

Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on 15 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Bacteria in warm food can double in twenty minutes. | TRUE ("doubling in number in as little as 20 minutes" between 40 °F and 140 °F, about 4–60 °C) | USDA FSIS, "How Temperatures Affect Food" — https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/how-temperatures-affect-food |
| 3 | US food rules: chill it within two hours. | TRUE ("Within 2 hours of cooking food … leftovers must be refrigerated"; 1 hour above 90 °F / 32 °C) | USDA FSIS, "Leftovers and Food Safety" (last updated 31 Jul 2020) — https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety ; "How Temperatures Affect Food" (1-hour rule) |
| 4 | Hot food can go straight in. | TRUE ("Hot food can be placed directly in the refrigerator or be rapidly chilled in an ice or cold water bath before refrigerating.") | USDA FSIS, "Leftovers and Food Safety" |
| 5 | Cool it first? That's a myth! | MYTH: waiting for food to cool on the counter is not required; it is the time in the 4–60 °C range that lets bacteria grow. | USDA FSIS (above) |
| 6 | Big pot? Divide it into shallow containers. | TRUE ("divide large amounts of food into shallow containers"; "A big pot of soup … will take a long time to cool, inviting bacteria to multiply") | USDA FSIS, "Leftovers and Food Safety"; NSW Food Authority, "Leftovers" (split into smaller portions; fridge within 2 hours) — https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/node/23948 |

Caveats said in plain words (description): other agencies (NSW Food Authority, UK FSA) phrase it as "cool and refrigerate within 2 hours" — the common rule is the 2-hour limit, not waiting until the food is cold. A very large hot pot can warm a small fridge, which is why big amounts should be split into shallow containers (or cooled in a cold-water bath) first.
