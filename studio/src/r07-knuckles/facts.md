# R07 · Does cracking your knuckles cause arthritis? — facts (checked 2026-11-12)

Education, not medical advice. Script is fixed (owner recording): plan/voice/R07.txt, copied unchanged to voice.txt.

| Line in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Does cracking your knuckles give you arthritis?" | The belief on trial (hook) | Common belief. |
| "Doctors X-rayed the hands of 215 older adults." | Confirmed (wording note) | deWeber, Olszewski & Ortolano 2011, J Am Board Fam Med 24(2):169-174 (abstract read on jabfm.org): 215 respondents aged 50-89; hand osteoarthritis confirmed on right-hand radiographs taken 2003-2008. The X-rays came from their medical records rather than being taken for the study; "X-rayed" is a fair plain-words summary. |
| "Crackers and non-crackers had arthritis at about the same rate." | Confirmed | Same study: OA in any joint 18.1% of crackers vs 21.5% of non-crackers (P = .548); "does not seem to be a risk factor for hand OA". |
| "One doctor cracked only one hand for sixty years." | Confirmed | Donald L. Unger, letter, Arthritis & Rheumatism 1998;41(5):949-950 (cracked the left hand at least twice a day for 50 years, right hand never); 2009 Ig Nobel Prize in Medicine citation: "every day for more than sixty (60) years" (improbable.com). Sixty follows the 2009 citation. |
| "Neither hand got arthritis. So far, it is a myth!" | Confirmed | Unger 1998: no arthritis in either hand, no difference between them (as reported by The Conversation and Healthline). "So far" keeps it honest: one person is an anecdote; the 2011 study is the real evidence. |
| "The pop is just a gas bubble forming in the joint." | Confirmed | Kawchuk et al. 2015, PLOS ONE 10(4):e0119470: real-time MRI showed the crack is the moment a gas-filled cavity forms in the joint fluid (ScienceDaily / EurekAlert release, University of Alberta). |

Caveat for the description: no study has linked knuckle cracking to arthritis, but if cracking a joint hurts or it swells, see a doctor.
