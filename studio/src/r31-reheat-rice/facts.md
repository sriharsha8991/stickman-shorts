# R31 · Can reheating leftover rice make you sick? — facts (verified 17 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **HALF TRUE** — reheated rice can make you sick, but the reheating is not the cause. Germs called *Bacillus
cereus* survive cooking as spores; if cooked rice sits warm for hours they grow and can make a toxin that reheating
does not destroy. Rice that was cooled fast, kept in the fridge and eaten within a day is safe to reheat once, until
steaming hot. Education, not medical advice.

| 1 | Hook: "Leftover rice? Before you reheat, can it make you sick?" | question | — |
| 2 | Rice germs survive cooking as tough spores | TRUE | Food Standards Scotland, "Rice": uncooked rice can contain *Bacillus cereus*; "its spores can survive being cooked". | 3 | Left warm for hours, they make a toxin | TRUE | FSS "Rice": the longer cooked rice is left at room temperature, the more likely the bacteria produce a toxin that makes it unsafe. FSAI factsheet: vegetative cells grow from 4 °C to 55 °C (optimum 30–40 °C); risk of growth and toxin production if rice is not cooled and stored correctly. |
| 4 | Reheating cannot destroy that toxin (card: 126 °C · 90 min, the toxin still survives) | TRUE | FSAI factsheet §3–4: the emetic toxin (cereulide) "is extremely stable and cannot be inactivated by reheating the food"; it "can survive at 126ºC for 90 minutes". |
| 5 | Reheated rice is risky? Half true! | TRUE (as HALF TRUE) | Combination of the above: risk comes from rice left warm before chilling; FSS: cold cooked rice is safe if cooled quickly, stored in the fridge and eaten within 24 hours. |
| 6 | The danger is the wait. Fridge it within an hour. (card: eat in 24 h · reheat once, piping hot) | TRUE | FSS "Rice": chill cooked rice "as quickly as possible, ideally within 1 hour", then straight into the fridge; eat within 24 hours; reheat until steaming hot all the way through; "Never reheat rice more than once". |

Caveats in the description (plain words): the toxin causes vomiting within a few hours (FSAI: emetic illness within about
five hours); most cases are mild but it can be serious; cooling faster is easier in a shallow container; freezing only
pauses growth. FSAI's general advice is to get cooked food into the fridge within two hours; the rice-specific advice
from FSS (and the NHS) is the stricter "ideally within one hour", which is what the Short says.
