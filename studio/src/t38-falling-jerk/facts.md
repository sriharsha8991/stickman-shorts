# T38 facts (checked 2026-11-07)

| Claim in the voice or on screen | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "You are almost asleep, and you feel yourself fall. Your whole body jumps, and you are awake." | Confirmed. AASM: sleep starts are sudden jerks as you fall asleep; "Some people also have a sensation of falling." | American Academy of Sleep Medicine, Sleep Education, "Sleep starts": https://sleepeducation.org/sleep-starts/ |
| "Most people feel this, and it is normal!" (small line: "Sleep doctors (AASM): 60 to 70% of people get these sleep starts.") | Confirmed. AASM: "Sleep starts are very common", "a fairly universal part of the process of falling asleep", occur in "60% to 70% of people", usually only now and then; "do not require treatment in most people". Sleep Foundation: "completely normal and generally harmless". | AASM page above; Sleep Foundation: https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/hypnic-jerks |
| "But look, your bed did not move." | Illustration of the falling feeling: nothing moved, the feeling came from the body/brain. Consistent with both sources (a sensation, not a real fall). | as above |
| "Too much coffee, and stress, can make it worse." (small line adds: hard exercise late, too little sleep) | Confirmed. AASM: frequency and intensity "can be increased by" high caffeine or stimulant intake, intense physical work or exercise beforehand, sleep deprivation, emotional stress. | AASM page above |
| Small line: "Strong or frequent jerks that worry you? Ask a doctor." | Caveat kept. AASM: possible to be injured (bruise, kicking a partner); frequent or intense jerks can cause fear of falling asleep, anxiety and sleep loss. | AASM page above |
| Nobody knows exactly why (caveat, on screen) | Confirmed. Sleep Foundation: "Researchers don't know for certain what causes hypnic jerks". AASM names no cause. | Sleep Foundation page above |
