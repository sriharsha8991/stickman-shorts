# T34 facts (checked 2026-11-08)

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In a big survey, most people had a song stuck in their head every week. | Confirmed: online survey of 12,519 Finnish internet users, 89.2% at least once a week. Self-selected sample, so "most people in the survey", said as "in a big survey". | Liikkanen 2012, Psychology of Music 40(2) (first online 27 Jun 2011), abstract: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735611406578 |
| In lab tests, chewing gum made the song come back less. | Confirmed: Beaman, Powell & Rapley 2015, QJEP 68(6):1049–1057, read in full. Exp 1 (44 students): gum cut the self-reported times the tune came to mind, F(1,43)=9.23, p=.004. Exp 2 (18): gum cut the reports, mostly "hearing the music". Exp 3 (36): gum gave fewer reports than finger tapping; tapping vs control not significant (gum vs control not tested separately). | https://core.ac.uk/download/42151264.pdf ; University of Reading release via ScienceDaily 28 Apr 2015: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/04/150428082010.htm |
| The tests were small and quick. | Confirmed: 98 students in total; 2–3 minute periods; one tune per experiment (two different tunes across the three). | same paper |
| Row line "It was a small test with only one song." | Adjusted: there were three tests and two tunes, so "But those tests were small, and quick." | same paper |

Not shown: the song names in the paper (commercial songs). The tune in the Short is an invented original. "Not a cure" on screen.
