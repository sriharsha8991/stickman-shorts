# R22 · Do blue-light glasses really protect your eyes? — facts (verified 2026-11-14)

Verdict: **MYTH** — there is no good evidence that blue-light filtering glasses protect your eyes from screens:
the largest review found they probably make no difference to eye strain from computer use, and no evidence about
protecting the retina; the American Academy of Ophthalmology says screen blue light has not been shown to damage eyes
and does not recommend blue-light glasses for computer use. Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on the day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Seventeen trials tested these glasses. | TRUE | Singh S, Keller PR, Busija L, McMillan P, Makrai E, Lawrenson JG, Hull CC, Downie LE. "Blue-light filtering spectacle lenses for visual performance, sleep, and macular health in adults." Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2023, CD013244.pub2 (published 18 Aug 2023). University of Melbourne / EurekAlert press release 17 Aug 2023 (https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/998545): 17 randomised controlled trials from six countries, 5–156 participants each, follow-up under a day to five weeks. |
| 3 | They probably didn't help tired eyes. | TRUE | Same review: blue-light filtering lenses "may not" give a short-term advantage over standard lenses for eye strain (visual fatigue) from computer use; "probably make no difference". |
| 4 | Sunlight has far more blue light than screens. | TRUE | Same press release: screens and other artificial lights give the eye about a thousandth of the blue light received from natural daylight. |
| 5 | Protecting your eyes? A myth! | TRUE | Review: no evidence of protection of the macula / retina (studies did not measure it; no conclusions on long-term retinal health). American Academy of Ophthalmology, "Should you be worried about blue light?" (aao.org, 2021, updated 2023): "there is no scientific evidence that blue light from digital devices causes damage to your eye"; "The Academy does not recommend any special blue light-blocking eyewear for computer use." |
| 6 | Screens tire your eyes because you blink less. Look away often. | TRUE | AAO same page: the discomfort is most likely digital eye strain; "Most of us blink less when looking at screens"; 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds). |

Caveats said in plain words / in the description:
- The trials were small and short (up to five weeks); the review calls the evidence uncertain. Long-term retinal effects were not studied, so "no evidence of protection", not "proven useless forever".
- Effects on sleep were unclear in the review; the Short only talks about eye protection and tired eyes.
- Blue-light lenses typically filter about 10–25% of blue light (press release); the glasses in line 3's photo were sold as cutting 52% (photographer's caption), not a claim we make.
