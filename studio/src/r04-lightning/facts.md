# R04 · Does lightning never strike the same place twice? — facts (checked 2026-11-11)

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Do you believe lightning never strikes one place twice? Why?" | The belief on trial (hook) | Common saying; the US National Weather Service lists it first on its "Lightning Myths" page. |
| "The Empire State Building is hit about twenty-three times a year." | Confirmed | NWS, "Lightning Myths": "The Empire State Building is hit an average of 23 times a year." (NOAA's "5 striking facts versus myths about lightning" says "about 25 times per year"; the Short uses the NWS figure with "about".) |
| "Tall towers get hit again and again." | Confirmed | NWS: "Lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly, especially if it's a tall, pointy, isolated object." NOAA: lightning "can, and often does, strike the same place repeatedly — especially if it's a tall and isolated object." |
| "Trees get struck many times a year." | Confirmed | NWS: natural objects that are tall and isolated, "like trees and mountains get struck by lightning many times a year." (Read as: trees as a group, not one tree.) |
| "That saying is a myth!" | Confirmed | NWS lists it as a myth (above). |
| "Hear thunder? Go indoors, not under a tree." | Confirmed (safety advice) | NWS slogan "When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors"; NWS lightning myths: sheltering under trees is dangerous (a tree is a likely strike target). |

Caveat for the description: a single tree is not hit every year; "many times a year" is NWS's wording for tall isolated objects in general.

Links read on 2026-11-11: https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths · https://www.noaa.gov/stories/5-striking-facts-versus-myths-about-lightning-you-should-know
