# H12 · The if-then trick — fact check (2026-10-21)

| Claim (as used) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| If-then plans (implementation intentions) hand control to a situational cue, so the cue triggers the action without a fresh decision | Confirmed | Gollwitzer & Oettingen (2013) review: "delegating action control to situational cues", planners "do not need to consciously intend to act in the critical moment" — https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/gollwitzer_oettingen_2013-Implementation_Intentions.pdf |
| "Across ninety-four tests: a medium-to-large boost" | Confirmed — Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis, Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych. 38: 94 independent tests, d = .65 on goal attainment (the 2013 review quotes d = .61) | https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f |
| "In one study, 91% exercised vs about one in three without a plan" | Confirmed — Milne, Orbell & Sheeran (2002), Br J Health Psych 7:163-184, N = 248 students: 91% (motivation + if-then plan) vs 38% control and 35% motivation-only | https://www.academia.edu/13555694 (full paper) ; doi:10.1348/135910702169420 |
| Caveat: mostly short-term, self-reported | Kept on screen and in script ("A nudge, not magic") | Row caveat; Milne 2002 used self-reported exercise with a short follow-up |
