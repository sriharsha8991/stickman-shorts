# R18 · Does a bag of rice really dry a wet phone? — facts (verified 2026-11-13)

Verdict: **MYTH** (rice is not a good way to dry a wet phone; open air did better in the one published comparison, and
Apple tells owners not to use rice).

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | "A test dried wet sponges in seven materials." | True | Gazelle, "Gazelle's guide to water damage: the truth about rice, Galaxy and everything" (6 May 2014), test 2: wet sponges left 24 h in seven materials (cat litter, pearl couscous, classic oatmeal, instant oatmeal, instant rice, silica gel, uncooked rice) plus one in open air. https://www.gazelle.com/thehorn/2014/05/06/gazelles-guide-water-damage-truth-rice-galaxy-everything/ (read 2026-11-13; the article lists seven materials although one sentence says six) |
| 3 | "Raw rice soaked up the least water." | True | Same Gazelle test: uncooked conventional rice was the worst of the seven, absorbing the least water. Repeated by iFixit, "Rice is for dinner, not repair" https://www.ifixit.com/News/30047 ("Dry, uncooked conventional rice was the worst of the seven options we tested"). No gram figures published, so the script gives none. |
| 4 | "Apple says rice bits can harm your phone." | True | Apple Support, "If you see a liquid-detection alert on your iPhone" (updated Feb 2024): "Don't put your iPhone in a bag of rice. Doing so could allow small particles of rice to damage your iPhone." The apple.com page could not be fetched from this sandbox (permission timeout); the exact wording was checked in two independent reports quoting it: TechSpot https://www.techspot.com/news/101961-apple-advises-against-using-rice-dry-wet-iphones.html and Popular Science https://www.popsci.com/technology/apple-iphone-rice-disproven/ |
| 6 | "Open air did better, so tap out water and wait." | True (plain words) | Gazelle: the open-air sponge "performed far better than any of the drying agents". Apple: tap the iPhone gently against your hand with the connector facing down, then leave it in a dry area with some airflow; wait at least 30 minutes, up to a day (same Apple page as line 4, via TechSpot). |

Caveat (plain words, in the description): the test used sponges, not phones; it shows rice is a poor drier, not that air
always saves a phone. A water-damaged phone may still need a repair shop. Not a substitute for the maker's instructions.
