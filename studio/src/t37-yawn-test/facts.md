# T37 facts (checked 2026-11-05)
| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In one big study, about two people in three yawned. | Confirmed: 222 of 328 healthy volunteers (67.7%) yawned at least once during a 183-second video of people yawning. Read in full on PMC. On screen: "222 of 328 yawned at a 3-minute yawn video". | Bartholomew & Cirulli 2014, PLoS ONE 9(3) e91773: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3954725 ; Duke Health release: https://corporate.dukehealth.org/news/contagious-yawning-may-not-be-linked-empathy-still-largely-unexplained |
| Many people say it shows you care. | Popular claim, kept as "many people say", not as a study finding. | Discussed as a debated idea in Massen, Church & Gallup 2015 (below) |
| But the proof is weak. | Confirmed: in the 2014 study empathy showed no effect once age was counted; age explained only about 8% of the variation. The 2017 review calls the evidence "inconsistent and inconclusive". Not "false": the heart goes soft, it does not pop. On screen: "The studies do not agree". | Bartholomew & Cirulli 2014; Massen & Gallup 2017, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 80:573-585: https://dspace.sunyconnect.suny.edu/items/a11f4777-14b6-4206-a8f9-4200d65e3e33 (abstract level, full paper not read) |
| Not everyone yawns, and some yawns just happen. | Confirmed: 106 of 328 did not yawn. 18 of 55 people who only heard breathing sounds still reported yawning (self-reports, small study). | Bartholomew & Cirulli 2014; Massen, Church & Gallup 2015, Front Psychol 6:1735: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4636538/ |

Not claimed: that this Short will make the viewer yawn (only asked). Study length differs: 3 minutes there, about 3 seconds here, so the viewer's chance is not the study's two in three; the on-screen line gives the 3-minute length.
