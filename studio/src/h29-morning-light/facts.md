# H29 · Does your alarm set your body clock? — facts (checked 2026-11-01)

Risk flag: Health-edu → "Education, not medical advice" on screen (with the source line, after 10 s); no cures or treatment claims; the camping result is framed as one small study.

| Claim (as said / shown) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A clock inside your head tells your body when to sleep and wake (body clock = circadian rhythm, run by the SCN "master clock"), and light influences it | confirmed | NIGMS circadian rhythms page: https://www.nigms.nih.gov/node/122896 |
| "It runs a little slow, so it needs a reset" — the human clock's natural cycle is slightly longer than a day (average 24 h 11 min), so it must be re-set each day | confirmed | Czeisler et al. 1999, Science 284:2177 (NIH/NIA release: https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/1999-06/NIoA-EtrR-250699.php; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10381883/) |
| "Your alarm cannot reset it, but light can" — light is the signal that shifts the clock; light after the body's low point moves it earlier, light before it moves it later | confirmed (sound is not a known clock-setting signal for humans; the line contrasts the alarm with light) | Khalsa et al. 2003, J Physiol 549:945 (PMC2342968); Wright et al. 2013 |
| "Scientists sent people camping for a week without lamps" — 8 adults (6 men, 2 women, mean age 30), one week in the Colorado wilderness, natural light only (sun + campfires), after a week of normal life | confirmed | Wright et al. 2013, Current Biology 23:1554 (https://www.Gwern.net/doc/melatonin/2013-wright.pdf); CU Boulder release via ScienceDaily: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/08/130801125601.htm |
| "Their body clocks moved about two hours earlier" — "all measured markers of internal circadian time were ~2 hr earlier" | confirmed | Wright et al. 2013 Results |
| "Now they were ready to wake at sunrise" — biological night began near sunset and ended just after sunrise, before wake time | confirmed (simplified) | Wright et al. 2013 Summary |
| "It was a small study, but lab tests agree" — controlled lab light studies show morning light moves the clock earlier | confirmed | Khalsa et al. 2003 (phase response curve) |
| Advice: open the curtains / get daylight after you wake | general education, not a treatment claim | Wright 2013; Khalsa 2003 |

Dropped / kept out:
- Slate payoff "Morning light sets your body clock" kept. Slate hook "Your alarm isn't what wakes your brain" softened to "Your alarm is not what sets your body clock" (the alarm does wake you; the true point is that it doesn't set the clock).
- Burns et al. 2023 Nature Mental Health (86,772 UK Biobank adults: more daytime light linked to lower, night light to higher risk of depression etc.) left out of the Short (one idea, max two numbers; association only). Kept here as background.
- Winter camping (5 people, >2.5 h earlier; Stothard et al. 2017) not used.
- No grogginess / energy / mood claims: the camping paper did not measure grogginess.
