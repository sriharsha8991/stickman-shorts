# R01 · Can you see the Great Wall of China from space? — facts (checked 2026-11-09, re-check on the production day)

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The wall generally can't be seen with the unaided eye from low Earth orbit | Confirmed | NASA, "China's Wall Less Great in View from Space" (2005, archived): "generally isn't, at least to the unaided eye in low Earth orbit" — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917153200/http://www.nasa.gov/vision/space/workinginspace/great_wall.html |
| "China's first astronaut could not find it." | Confirmed | Same NASA page: Yang Liwei "couldn't see the historic structure"; Scientific American: he admitted he had not seen it after Shenzhou 5 (2003) — https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/is-chinas-great-wall-visible-from-space/ |
| "Its colour matches the land around it." | Confirmed | Scientific American quoting former astronaut Jeffrey Hoffman: its colour is "not that different from the ground on either side of it" |
| "Only a camera, with a long lens, captured it." | Confirmed with care | NASA image ISS010-E-08497 (24 Nov 2004, Leroy Chiao): "apparently the first verifiable photo showing the Great Wall of China from low earth orbit" (https://images.nasa.gov/details/iss010e08497). The lens is given as 400 mm in the image caption and 180 mm in the 2005 article, so the Short says only "a long lens". Chiao himself was not sure he had seen it. |
| "Quiz books and classrooms say yes." | Framing of a common belief | The "only man-made object visible from space" claim is widely repeated (Scientific American). |

No real person is shown in a photo: Yang Liwei appears only as words on a card.
