# V032 facts (checked 2026-11-04)

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shoppers give too much attention to the first number on the left of a price. | Confirmed. | Manning & Sprott press release via EurekAlert (University of Chicago Press Journals, 2009): https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2009-02/uocp-btl022309.php · ScienceDaily: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/02/090223221526.htm |
| A price ending in nine looks smaller than a price one cent higher when the first number changes ($2.99 vs $3.00). | Confirmed, with a limit: only when the left digit changes, and it depends on how close the prices are. | Thomas & Morwitz 2005, Journal of Consumer Research 32(1):54–64: https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v32y2005i1p54-64.html · ScienceDaily: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2005/06/050607030351.htm |
| Pen test: $2.00 and $3.99 → 44% chose the higher-priced pen; $1.99 and $4.00 → 18%. | Confirmed by the journal's press release and WSU. Full-text paper not read; sample size not stated in those sources, so it is not shown. | Manning & Sprott 2009, J Consumer Research (DOI 10.1086/597215): https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-597215.html · WSU: https://news.wsu.edu/?p=81491 |
| "Both prices moved by just one cent." | Confirmed: $2.00 → $1.99 and $3.99 → $4.00. Both first numbers change (2 → 1 and 3 → 4). | Same. |
| ₹199 vs ₹200 "feels much cheaper". | Adjusted: our own rupee example of the same left-digit change, not a study price. Labelled on screen. Plan's ₹99 vs ₹100 changed to ₹199 vs ₹200 because the study pattern is a same-length price with a different first digit. | Thomas & Morwitz 2005 (mechanism). |
| It does not always happen. | Kept on screen ("One test with pens. It does not happen every time."). The same paper found round vs just-below prices made no difference for a gift for a very close friend or for purchases of only a few dollars. | ScienceDaily 2009, above. |
