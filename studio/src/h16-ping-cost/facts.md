# H16 facts (checked 2026-11-02)

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The popular "23 minutes after an interruption" number exists and is real, but it measures the time until people return to the interrupted work | Confirmed. Gloria Mark, interview, 8 June 2006: interrupted work was "resumed, on average, in 23 minutes and 15 seconds" (work resumed the same day, 81.9% of cases) | https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/23146/Too-Many-Interruptions-Work.aspx |
| "After a ping, people often do two other jobs." | Confirmed, simplified. Same interview: "about two intervening tasks". CHI 2005 field study (24 information workers: 7 managers, 9 analysts, 8 developers): 2.26 other working spheres on average before resuming | Gallup above; https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI05-p321-mark.pdf |
| "about 23 minutes later" | Confirmed with "about". The paper's own figures: 25 min 26 s overall, 22 min 37 s for external interruptions, 29 min 1 s for self-interruptions; 77.2% resumed the same day. 23:15 is the interview figure | Mark, Gonzalez & Harris, CHI 2005 (above); Fast Company interview: https://www.fastcompany.com/944128/worker-interrupted-cost-task-switching |
| The number does not measure lost concentration | Confirmed (context, not spoken as a study result): the study measured when work resumed, not recovery of focus | https://scienceblog.com/t-23-minutes-return-focus-after-interruption/ (secondary; the 2005 paper's method agrees) |
| "In a test, two groups did the same email job. Only one group got interrupted." | Adjusted to plain words. Mark, Gudith & Klocke, CHI 2008: 48 people (81% German university students) answered emails in a role-play; interruptions were phone calls and instant messages; conditions were no interruption vs two interruption conditions (same vs different context), merged here as "interrupted" | https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI08.pdf |
| "The interrupted group finished faster." | Confirmed for this test. Baseline 22.77 min vs 20.31 and 20.60 min. Times not shown on screen (max two numbers, and 22.77 would be confused with 23) | same |
| "Their work was just as good." | Confirmed for this test: no significant difference in errors or politeness. (Emails were shorter in the interrupted conditions; left out, one idea) | same |
| "But they felt more stress." | Confirmed: significantly higher stress, frustration and effort in both interruption conditions | same |
| "try one hour with no pings" | Our tip, not a study finding. Said on screen: "A tip to try, not a study result" | n/a |

**Caveats kept:** on screen during the test room: "one small test: 48 people, mostly students"; the gauges are labelled an illustration (the paper reports scores, not a dial). The spoken line says "In a test". The Short does not claim that interruptions never cost time in real work.

**Hook:** "Did you just lose 23 minutes?" is a question, not a claim; the Short answers it honestly. Gloria Mark is named only in the small source line, not in the hook or title.

**Note on a common error:** the 23-minute figure does not appear in the 2008 paper (confirmed on the PDF); many articles cite it wrongly.
