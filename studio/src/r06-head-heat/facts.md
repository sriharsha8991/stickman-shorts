# R06 · Do you lose most of your heat through your head? — facts (checked 2026-11-12)

Education, not medical advice.

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Before grabbing a hat, does your head lose most heat?" | The belief on trial (hook) | Common belief; the 1970 US Army survival manual FM 21-76 (p. 148) said 40 to 45 percent of body heat is lost through the head (quoted in Vreeman & Carroll 2008). |
| "Your head is only seven percent of your skin." | Confirmed | Pretorius, Bristow, Steinman & Giesbrecht 2006, J Appl Physiol 101:669-675 (abstract + discussion read): the head is "7% of the body surface area". |
| "Scientists measured heads in cold water." | Confirmed | Same study: 8 healthy men in 17 °C water, body insulated or not, head above the water or fully submerged, shivering blocked with drugs; heat loss measured. |
| "Heads lost only one tenth of the heat." | Confirmed | Same study: with the body exposed, submerging the head increased total heat loss by only 10%; the head accounted for about 10% of total heat loss. |
| "Most heat through the head? A myth!" | Confirmed | Vreeman & Carroll, "Festive medical myths", BMJ 2008;337:a2769: the belief is a myth; any uncovered part of the body loses heat, roughly in proportion. |
| "An army test left only heads uncovered." | Confirmed | Vreeman & Carroll 2008 (citing O'Connor, NYT 2004): in an old military study subjects wore arctic survival suits but no hats, so they lost most heat through their heads only because it was the only bare part. No year given in the BMJ paper, so no year is said in the Short. |

Caveat for the description: a hat still helps on a cold day, because any bare skin loses heat. The head is not special; it is just often the only part left uncovered.
