# T35 facts (checked 2026-11-07)

| Claim on screen or in the voice | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Most adults feel this, about once every week." | Confirmed. Brown (1991) review: TOTs "occur about once a week", increase with age; self-estimates in adults "approximately once a week"; diary studies one to two a week for younger adults, two to four for older adults. Small line on screen: "Most adults say it happens about once a week (review of studies, 1991)." | Brown, A. S. (1991), Psychological Bulletin 109(2):204–223, full text: https://core.ac.uk/download/208321001.pdf |
| "Later, the same word tends to hide again." | Confirmed. D'Angelo & Humphreys (2015), 6 experiments: a word that caused a TOT was likely to cause one again on a later test (5 min, 48 h or a week later). Also Warriner & Humphreys (2008, QJEP, 30 students). | Abstract: https://experts.mcmaster.ca/display/publication1426228 ; BPS Research Digest: https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/why-words-get-stuck-tip-your-tongue-and-how-stop-it-recurring ; https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/why-same-words-keep-getting-stuck-tip-your-tongue |
| "But when people found it themselves, it hid less often!" | Confirmed. Abstract: error learning "can be corrected when individuals resolve their TOT, making them less likely to re-experience a TOT for that word on a later test." BPS: being told the word did not stop the repeat. Oliver & Humphreys 2019 (Frontiers) restate it: less likely to repeat if the TOT was self-resolved (0.15 vs 0.41 in their own data, not shown). | Same as above; https://frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00341/full |
| "So ask for the first letter, then find the word." | Supported, lab only. Abstract: "orthographic cues can help individuals resolve their TOTs, and that these cued-resolutions lead to corrective learning in much the same way as self-resolutions." BPS: first few letters given prevented the repeat. Small line on screen: "In the tests, first-letter hints helped too. A small help, not a cure." | Abstract above |

Caveat kept (row): lab tasks with rare words (on screen: "Lab tests, 2015: people found hard, rare words…"); not a cure (on screen).
Note: Humphreys' own advice in the 2008 BPS piece is different in detail (look the word up fast and say it to yourself; stop straining). Our tip follows the 2015 finding on letter cues; we do not tell people to strain for a long time.
The full 2015 paper was not read (abstract + two secondary summaries). Sample sizes not shown.
Row changes: hook "Why does 1 word hide when you need it?" (50) → "Why does your memory hide one word you know?" (56). "Almost everyone gets this, often about once a week" → "Most adults feel this, about once every week" (Brown: adults' self-estimates). "It can come back with that same word later" → "Later, the same word tends to hide again" (the pronoun was unclear). "Finding it yourself makes it less likely to return!" → "But when people found it themselves, it hid less often!" (past tense: what the test found). "So try the first letter before you search online" → "So ask for the first letter, then find the word" (the study gave letter cues; it did not test guessing letters alone or searching online).
The stapler, the calendar mark and the trophy card are illustrations, not data.
