# Why you can't stop scrolling — storyboard

9:16 · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · 1080 frames (36 s) · no voiceover: kinetic word captions carry the script.
Music 120 BPM → 1 beat = 15 frames, 1 bar = 60 frames; every scene starts on a bar line.

## Direction (motion-art-direction)
- Tone cell: kinetic-soft → **Playful** personality. Entrances snap with overshoot (engine SNAP),
  exits ease-in, base unit 6 frames, stagger 3 frames per caption word.
- Transitions: hard cuts with a punch-in zoom on every scene start; one wipe only (night → day).
- One "wow": the phone turning into a slot machine (S3).
- One hero per frame: title zone (y 260–620) · action zone (y 650–1290) · caption zone (y ~1400).
- Pattern interrupt every 2–4 s (uneven): slams, punch-ins, stamps, counters, the reveal.

## Hook (storytelling-hooks: three layers)
- First frame: night, YOU sitting up in bed, face lit by phone, clock 2:47.
- Text overlay: "2:47 AM." / "STILL SCROLLING." (emotional mirror)
- Caption line (scored 59 WORKABLE, Direct Address): "If you lose hours to your feed every night… one
  2006 design decision is why." (curiosity gap + stakes)

## Facts (sourced)
- Aza Raskin designed infinite scroll in 2006; he has estimated it wastes 200,000+ human lifetimes a day, and has said he regrets it.
- Loren Brichter, inventor of pull-to-refresh, told The Guardian (2017): "Pull-to-refresh is addictive."
- Unpredictable (variable-ratio) rewards drive the most compulsive checking — the slot-machine principle (Skinner).
