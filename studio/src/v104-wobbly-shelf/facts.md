| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |

| "Scientists gave people a flat-pack box. Half built it. Half got it ready-made. The builders bid sixty-three percent more." | **Confirmed.** Exp. 1A: 52 students; builders assembled a plain black IKEA storage box, non-builders inspected a pre-assembled one. Bids $0.78 vs $0.48 (t(50) = 2.12, p < .05), a 63% premium. Builders also liked the box more (3.81 vs 2.50 / 7). On screen: "small student study". | Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012, *J. Consumer Psychology* 22(3):453–460 — author manuscript: https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/12136084/norton%20mochon%20ariely%20third%20round[1].pdf · SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1777100 |
| "Same box. Their effort was the price tag." | **Confirmed (paraphrase).** Identical boxes; the only difference was who assembled it. Authors: labour alone increases valuation. | Norton et al. 2012 |
| "Stop two steps early? Bids dropped to less than half." | **Confirmed.** Exp. 3: 39 participants; "incomplete builders" stopped before the last two steps (all parts present). Bids $0.59 vs $1.46 for those who finished (t(37) = 2.35, p < .05) → 40%, i.e. less than half. Authors: "labor leads to love only when labor results in successful completion of tasks." | Norton et al. 2012, Exp. 3 |
| "You love the finish, not the shelf." | **Kept as the authors' conclusion** (completion is required; Exp. 2: building then un-building Lego erased the effect). Phrased as a takeaway, not a new claim. | Norton et al. 2012, Exp. 2–3 |
