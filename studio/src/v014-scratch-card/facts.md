# V014 · Why payment apps give you scratch cards — facts (checked 2026-10-23, retention v2 cut)

| Claim (as used) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Payment-app scratch cards can reveal no reward ("Better Luck Next Time"); "Not all scratch cards guarantee a reward" → spoken "Even the rules say not every card wins" | Confirmed (app's own help page, re-fetched 2026-10-23) | https://paytm.com/support/rewards-cashback/what-is-a-paytm-scratch-card |
| UPI-app scratch-card payouts are randomised (some pay, some say "better luck next time"); a luck-based, gamified reward → "Some cards pay. Some don't. You never know which." | Confirmed (analysis) | Shashidhar K J, ORF, 28 Oct 2019: https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/the-weaponisation-of-cashbacks-on-upi-by-google-pay-57099 |
| Scratch cards are earned after eligible payments; offer T&Cs decide eligibility | Confirmed | https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/7580493 |
| 1950s pigeon experiments (Ferster & Skinner, *Schedules of Reinforcement*, 1957): food after an unpredictable number of pecks (variable ratio) → high, steady responding, most resistant to extinction → "pigeons got food after random pecks… kept them pecking the longest" | Confirmed (textbook summary) | OpenStax/Lumen Psychology, "Reinforcement Schedules": https://courses.lumenlearning.com/waymaker-psychology/chapter/reading-reinforcement-schedules |
| Slot machines / gambling are the textbook example of variable-ratio reinforcement → "That's a slot machine" (as a mechanism comparison, not a claim that the app is gambling) | Confirmed (same source) | as above |
| "You win almost nothing" (hook) | Viewer's experience framing, not a statistic; no odds or share of empty cards is shown (none published) | — |

Dropped / avoided: any odds, app names or logos on screen, any claim about an app's intent beyond its own rule that not every card wins. Not financial advice: no product recommendation; the takeaway is "pay for the thing, not the scratch".
