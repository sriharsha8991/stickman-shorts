# V014 · Why payment apps give you scratch cards — facts (checked 2026-10-23)

| Claim (as used) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Payment-app scratch cards can reveal no reward ("Better Luck Next Time"); "Not all scratch cards guarantee a reward" | Confirmed (app's own help page) | https://paytm.com/support/rewards-cashback/what-is-a-paytm-scratch-card |
| UPI app scratch-card payouts are randomised: some pay hundreds of rupees, some say "better luck next time"; described as a luck-based, gamified reward | Confirmed (analysis) | Shashidhar K J, ORF, 28 Oct 2019: https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/the-weaponisation-of-cashbacks-on-upi-by-google-pay-57099 |
| Scratch cards are earned after eligible payments; offer T&Cs decide eligibility | Confirmed | https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/7539588 |
| 1950s pigeon experiments (Ferster & Skinner, *Schedules of Reinforcement*, 1957): rewards after an unpredictable number of responses (variable ratio) give high, steady responding and are the most resistant to extinction (keep responding after rewards stop) | Confirmed (textbook summary of Skinner) | Lumen/OpenStax Psychology, "Reinforcement Schedules": https://courses.lumenlearning.com/waymaker-psychology/chapter/reading-reinforcement-schedules |
| Gambling / slot machines are the classic example of variable-ratio reinforcement | Confirmed (same source; Skinner uses gambling as his example) | as above |
| "₹200" payment | Illustrative amount, not a claim | — |

Dropped / avoided: any odds or share of empty cards (not published), app names or logos on screen, claims about any app's intent beyond its own rule that not every card wins. The "hook" line is framed as the mechanism (uncertainty keeps you coming back), consistent with the reinforcement literature.
