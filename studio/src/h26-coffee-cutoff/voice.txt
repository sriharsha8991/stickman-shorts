Your three PM coffee could be stealing an hour of sleep.
And you wouldn't notice.
All day, a sleepy signal builds up: adenosine.
Caffeine plugs its receptors, so you don't feel it.
The catch: caffeine's half-life is about four hours.
For some people it's two. For others, eight.
So part of that cup is still in you at bedtime.
In a small study, twelve people took four hundred milligrams
six hours before bed.
They lost over an hour of sleep,
and mostly didn't notice.
That's a big dose, and bodies vary a lot.
So try a cut-off: last cup eight hours before bed.
Bed at eleven? Coffee by three.
