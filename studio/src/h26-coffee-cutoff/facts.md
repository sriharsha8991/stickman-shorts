# H26 · Your 3 PM coffee is stealing an hour of sleep — fact check (2026-10-22)

Risk flag: Health-edu → on screen "Education, not medical advice"; no cures or treatment claims; findings framed as one small study.

| Claim (as used) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "A sleepy signal builds up: adenosine" | Confirmed — adenosine "is thought to increase in the extracellular space in the course of prolonged waking", a proposed "homeostatic accumulator of the need to sleep" | Reichert, Deboer & Landolt (2022), J Sleep Res 31:e13597 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541543 |
| "Caffeine plugs its receptors, so you don't feel it" | Confirmed — "Caffeine is a potent adenosine receptor antagonist" | same |
| "Caffeine's half-life is about four hours; for some two, for others eight" | Confirmed — EFSA: "In healthy adults, the average half-life is approximately four hours, with a range of two to eight hours" | https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/corporate_publications/files/efsaexplainscaffeine150527.pdf |
| "So part of that cup is still in you at bedtime" | Follows from the half-life (after 8 h at a 4 h half-life ≈ a quarter remains); on screen shown as approximate | EFSA (above) |
| "In a small study, twelve people took 400 mg six hours before bed. They lost over an hour of sleep" | Confirmed — Drake et al. (2013), J Clin Sleep Med 9(11): 12 healthy normal sleepers, 400 mg at 0, 3 or 6 h before bed; at 6 h "objectively measured total sleep time was dramatically reduced (more than one hour)" | https://aasm.org/late-afternoon-and-early-evening-caffeine-can-disrupt-sleep-at-night/ |
| "…and mostly didn't notice" | Confirmed — "subjective reports suggest that participants were unaware of this sleep disturbance" (hedged as "mostly") | same |
| Caveat: "That's a big dose, and bodies vary a lot" | Kept (row caveat). AASM: 400 mg ≈ "two to three cups of coffee"; half-life range 2–8 h | AASM; EFSA |
| "Try a cut-off: last cup eight hours before bed. Bed at eleven? Coffee by three." | Suggestion framed as "try", from the row's payoff (8+ h); the AASM release says caffeine "generally should be avoided after 5 p.m." | AASM release |
| "Your three PM coffee could be stealing an hour of sleep" | Hedged hook ("could be"); the >1 h figure is from the 400 mg / 6 h condition | Drake 2013 |
