Miss ten thousand steps, and you feel like you failed.
But why that number? Not a study.
It likely came from a Japanese pedometer from nineteen sixty-five,
called manpo-kei: the ten-thousand-steps meter.
A catchy name became a health rule.
The science? A twenty twenty-five review of fifty-seven studies.
Seven thousand steps a day, versus two thousand,
was linked to forty-seven percent lower risk of early death.
Past that, the extra gains were modest.
So aim for about seven thousand. Every step still counts.
These are links, not guarantees.
And that goal you missed? Nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine...
