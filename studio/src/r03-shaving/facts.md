# R03 · Does shaving make hair grow back thicker? — facts (checked 2026-11-11)

Education, not medical advice.

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Before you shave again, does hair grow back thicker? Why?" | The belief on trial (hook) | Common belief; Mayo Clinic answers it directly (below). |
| "Researchers shaved one leg of five men, every week." | Confirmed (via a secondary source) | Lynfield, Y. L. & MacWilliams, P. (1970). Shaving and hair growth. *Journal of Investigative Dermatology* 55(3): 170–172. doi:10.1111/1523-1747.ep12280667 (PubMed 5459955; no abstract online). Design as described by *Scientific American* ("Fact or Fiction?: If You Shave or Wax, Your Hair Will Come Back Thicker"): five healthy young men shaved one leg weekly for several months, the other leg was the control. The full paper was not read. |
| "The other leg was never shaved." | Confirmed (same source) | The unshaved leg was the control. |
| "Both legs grew hair just as thin." | Confirmed (same source) | *Scientific American*: "no significant differences in the hair width, coarseness or rate of growth." The Short says only "just as thin" (width). |
| "So thicker hair is a myth!" | Confirmed | Mayo Clinic, "Shaving hair: Does shaved hair grow back thicker?": "No — shaving hair doesn't change its thickness, color or rate of growth." Also Trotter, M. (1928). Hair growth and shaving. *The Anatomical Record* 37(4): 373–379, doi:10.1002/ar.1090370405 (four men, shaved face hair measured: "no evidence that shaving accelerates the rate of beard growth", as quoted by Scientific American). |
| "A razor leaves a blunt tip, so stubble feels rough." | Confirmed | Mayo Clinic: "Shaving facial or body hair gives the hair a blunt tip. The tip might feel coarse or 'stubbly' for a time as it grows out." |

Caveat for the description: the 1970 study was small (five men, legs only). Regrowth can *look* darker for a while because the short stubs are not yet lightened by sun (not said in the Short).
