# R26 · Does your fridge waste your bread before you eat it? — facts (verified 16 Nov 2026, 00:00 UTC)

Food/everyday education only. The verdict is about staling (bread going hard and dry), not about food safety.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on 15–16 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does your fridge waste your bread before you eat it? | the question (the belief on trial: "the fridge keeps bread fresh longer") | — |
| 2 | A 2011 lab test kept bread three ways. | TRUE: bread stored at 25 °C, 4 °C and −18 °C; staling followed by water activity and X-ray diffraction (starch recrystallisation) | Aguirre, Osella, Carrara, Sánchez & Buera, "Effect of storage temperature on starch retrogradation of bread staling", Starch/Stärke 63(9):587–593 (2011), doi 10.1002/star.201100023 — https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df4ed90f3e94b9e5d73a52 ; http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12110/paper_00389056_v63_n9_p587_Aguirre |
| 3 | In the fridge, at four degrees, staling was fastest. | TRUE ("At 4°C the rate of starch retrogradation is the highest for the studied conditions") | Aguirre et al. 2011 (abstract) |
| 4 | In the freezer, staling almost stopped. | TRUE: at −18 °C water activity stayed almost constant for 23 days and only existing crystals grew (no new ones); household freezer temperatures are "sufficient to almost halt the recrystallization process" | Aguirre et al. 2011; D. Mercer (Univ. of Guelph), "The Complexities of Bread Staling", IUFoST — https://iufost.org/iufostold/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/66-The-Complexities-of-Bread-Staling.pdf |
| 5 | Stale faster in the fridge? True! | TRUE: "bread kept in the refrigerator actually stales more quickly than bread that is not refrigerated" (Mercer); "Storing in the refrigerator promotes staling" (Clemson Extension) | Mercer (IUFoST); Clemson Cooperative Extension, Home & Garden Information Center — https://hgic.clemson.edu/?p=3267 |
| 6 | Cold slows spoilage, so it feels right. Freeze slices instead. | TRUE: refrigeration "slows the spoilage process" (Mercer); freezing keeps bread about 3 months (Clemson) and nearly halts staling | Mercer (IUFoST); Clemson HGIC |
| 7 | Where do you keep your bread? | comment question | — |

Not used (could not trace to a primary source): the popular "six times faster in the fridge" figure.
Caveats said in plain words (description): staling is not spoilage. The fridge does slow spoilage, so in very hot,
humid weather, or for bread you will not finish in a few days, freeze it (slices toast straight from frozen; heating
softens stale bread for a while, Mercer). Exact speeds depend on the bread and the fridge.
