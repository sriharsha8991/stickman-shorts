# H20 · Is your phone making you less smart? — facts (checked 2026-10-30)

| Claim (as spoken) | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "In 2017, scientists gave people a memory test." | Confirmed. Ward, Duke, Gneezy & Bos (2017), "Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity", J. Assoc. Consumer Research 2(2):140. Nearly 800 smartphone users; working-memory capacity (OSpan) and fluid-intelligence tasks; phones silent, face down on the desk, in a pocket/bag, or in another room | https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/06/170623133039.htm ; https://doi.org/10.1086/691462 |
| "People with their phone in another room did better." | Confirmed (vs. phone on the desk; slightly better than pocket/bag) | same |
| "So people said phones make us less smart." | Confirmed as the popular framing ("brain drain"); not a claim of the study about IQ | press coverage 2017 (ScienceDaily, Medical News Today) |
| "But others repeated the test, and the difference was gone." | Confirmed for a preregistered direct replication of Ward et al. Exp. 2 (Acta Psychologica 230:103717, Oct 2022): no difference between phone locations on OSpan or Go/No-Go. Hack row also cites Hartmann et al. (N≈300) with no effect | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36007374/ |
| "Then a review looked at fifty-six studies together." | Confirmed. Parry (2024), "Does the Mere Presence of a Smartphone Impact Cognitive Performance? A Meta-Analysis of the 'Brain Drain Effect'", Media Psychology 27(5):737–762; k = 56, N = 7,093 | https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/does-the-mere-presence-of-a-smartphone-impact-cognitive-performan/ ; https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2023.2286647 |
| "It found only a small drop in short-term memory." | Confirmed in plain words: working-memory capacity was the only function with a significant (negative) pooled effect; fluid intelligence, sustained attention and response inhibition were null; author: "limited support for a negative albeit smaller than theorized brain drain effect", heterogeneity and low power. "Short-term memory" = plain words for working memory | Parry 2024 (above) |
| "So there may be an effect, but it is small." | Confirmed, matches both meta-analyses (also Böttger, Poschik & Zierer 2023, Behav. Sci. 13:751: overall g = −0.14, memory g = −0.23, attention n.s.) | https://mdpi.com/2076-328X/13/9/751 |
| "Back at your desk, put your phone in another room." | Advice, not a fact claim (free, low-cost; Böttger et al. also recommend keeping phones away while studying). The row's "removes the urge to check" is not stated as a fact | Böttger et al. 2023 |
