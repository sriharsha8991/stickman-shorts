| Scientists tested this with people reading words in turn. | Confirmed. 88 male University of Michigan undergraduates, 22 per session, seated round a large square of tables, read word cards aloud one by one (25 words per list), then wrote what they recalled. Paper read directly. On screen: "A 1973 test: 88 male students read words aloud in turn (Brenner)." | Brenner 1973, J Verbal Learning & Verbal Behavior 12(3):320-323: https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/33869/0000130.pdf;sequence=1 ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5371(73)80076-3 |
| They often forgot the words from just before their turn! | Adjusted from the row. The row said "far fewer words from the person before". The paper says: "performing subjects tended not to recall words read as many as three positions (9 sec) before and after performance", position effect F(9, 720) = 6.52, p < .001 for readers, negligible for listeners (F = .70). It gives no percentage per position (a graph only), so "far fewer" and any number for that position were dropped. "Just before their turn" covers more than one person. On screen: "Weak memory about 9 s before and after their own turn." | Brenner 1973 (as above) |
| Later tests agree (small line). | Confirmed at secondary level: Forrin et al. 2019 list replications in free recall, cued recall and recognition (Bond & Kirkpatrick 1982; Bond & Omar 1990; Brown & Oxman 1978; Innes 1982; Walker & Orr 1976; Bond 1985; Bond, Pitre & van Leeuwen 1991). Those papers were not read one by one. | Forrin et al. 2019, J Memory and Language: https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/jml19.pdf |
