# R02 · Do goldfish really forget in three seconds? — facts (checked 2026-11-10)

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Jokes say goldfish remember only three seconds." | Framing of a common belief | The "three-second memory" saying is widely repeated (e.g. Live Science, "Do goldfish really have a 3-second memory?"). Not a scientific claim. |
| "But in a lab, goldfish learned to press a lever." | Confirmed | Gee, P., Stephenson, D. & Wright, D. E. (1994). Temporal discrimination learning of operant feeding in goldfish (Carassius auratus). *Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior* 62(1): 1–13. doi:10.1901/jeab.1994.62-1 (University of Plymouth). Fish were trained to press a lever for food. |
| "The lever gave food, for one hour, each day." | Confirmed | Same paper: the feeding window was narrowed step by step to 1 hour in every 24 hours and kept there for four weeks. |
| "The fish learned that hour, and pressed the lever then." | Confirmed with care | Same paper: the fish's lever pressing showed they anticipated the feeding time (temporal discrimination); in Experiment 2 this also developed under constant light. Caveat (not in the script): when food was withheld for six days, the timed pattern lasted only a limited number of days. The Short says only that they learned the hour. |
| "So the three second memory is a myth!" | Confirmed | Learning a one-hour window in a 24-hour day needs memory across a day, far longer than three seconds. Fish researcher Culum Brown (Macquarie University) also says fish memories last weeks to months (Earth.com, 2024; Brown 2015, *Animal Cognition* 18:1–17, "Fish intelligence, sentience and ethics", doi:10.1007/s10071-014-0761-0). |
| "That needs a memory of a whole day." | Our reasoning from the study | The fish had to keep track of time over a 24-hour cycle to press at the right hour. Worded as "a whole day", not as a measured memory span. |

Checked but not used: the often-quoted "goldfish remember for three months" (Plymouth) comes from press reports, not a paper we could read, so the Short gives no month figure.
