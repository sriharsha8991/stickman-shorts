# R35 · Wet hair? Before you step out, will you catch cold? — facts (verified 18–19 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **HALF TRUE** — cold air and wet hair cannot give you a cold on their own: colds are caused by viruses
(CDC: more than 200 of them, rhinoviruses most often). In a 1968 NEJM trial, chilling volunteers who were given a cold
virus showed no effect on their colds ("failure to demonstrate effect"). But in a 2005 Cardiff trial (180 people),
13 of 90 who sat with their feet in cold water reported a cold in the next 4–5 days, against 5 of 90 who did not
(more than twice as many). The authors' explanation: chilling may let a virus you already carry take hold. Lab work
(Yale, PNAS 2015/2016) agrees on the mechanism: the common-cold virus copies better at nose temperature (33 °C)
than at body temperature (37 °C) because the cells' defences are weaker when cool.
Caveat in plain words: no virus, no cold. Wet hair itself has not been tested; the evidence is about chilling the body.
Education, not medical advice.
