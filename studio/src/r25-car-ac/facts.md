# R25 · Does your AC cost you more fuel than open windows? — facts (verified 15 Nov 2026)

Money/everyday education only.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on 15 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | In heat, AC can cut mileage by over a quarter. | TRUE ("AC use can reduce a conventional vehicle's fuel economy by more than 25%", in very hot conditions; the effect is even larger, in %, for hybrids and EVs) | US DOE / EPA, fueleconomy.gov, "Driving in hot weather" — https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/hotweather.shtml |
| 3 | Open windows cost about four to eight percent. | TRUE ("Driving with all four windows down decreased fuel economy by 4–8.5 percent for the Corolla and 1–4 percent for the Explorer") | Oak Ridge National Laboratory (US DOE) news release, 9 Apr 2014 — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/630158 ; Huff, West & Thomas, "Effects of Air Conditioner Use on Real-World Fuel Economy", SAE Int. J. Passeng. Cars 2013, doi 10.4271/2013-01-0551 — https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1092236 |
| 4 | At highway speed, they (open windows) add drag. | TRUE ("Open windows increase wind resistance … quite small at low speeds but increases at highway speeds") | fueleconomy.gov, "Driving in hot weather" |
| 5 | AC burns more fuel? Half true! | HALF TRUE: in the ORNL test, at steady 40–70 mph with the AC on full cooling, both cars used more fuel with AC than with windows down; with the AC at half load, the Corolla used more fuel with windows down above about 60 mph (~100 km/h), and with full AC the windows-down cost matched AC at 75 mph and passed it at 80 mph. So AC usually costs more, but at highway speeds the gap closes or flips, depending on car and AC load. | DOE VTO "Fact #990, August 14, 2017: Comparison of Vehicle Efficiencies Using the Air Conditioner versus Windows Down" — https://www.energy.gov/node/2698173 ; OSTI abstract (above) |
| 6 | In town, windows down. On highways, use AC. | TRUE (official tip: "Roll the windows down at lower speeds; use the AC at highway speeds.") | fueleconomy.gov, "Driving in hot weather" |

Caveats said in plain words (description): results come from US tests of two 2009 cars; the exact crossover speed depends on the car, how hard the AC works and the heat. Safety and comfort come first: in heavy heat or dust, use the AC.
