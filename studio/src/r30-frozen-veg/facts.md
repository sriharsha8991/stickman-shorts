# R30 · Are frozen vegetables less healthy than fresh? — facts (verified 17 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **MYTH** — frozen vegetables are not less healthy than fresh ones in general. In a two-year lab comparison most
vitamin levels in fresh and frozen produce were the same, and fresh produce kept five days in the fridge sometimes had
less than frozen. Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: "Frozen vegetables? Before you skip them, are they worse?" | question | — |
| 2 | A two-year test checked eight foods | TRUE | Li, Pegg, Eitenmiller, Chun & Kerrihard 2017 (J Food Compos Anal, doi:10.1016/j.jfca.2017.02.002): broccoli, cauliflower, corn, green beans, green peas, spinach, blueberries, strawberries; vitamin C, trans-β-carotene, total folate; a two-year study (Columbia TC Tisch Center summary). |
| 3 | Most vitamins came out the same | TRUE | Li et al. 2017 abstract (Montclair Digital Commons): most comparisons showed no significant difference between fresh and frozen; authors: results do not support the belief that fresh is significantly more nutritious than frozen. |
| 4 | After five fridge days, fresh sometimes fell behind | TRUE | Li et al. 2017: "fresh-stored" = refrigerated 5 days to mimic home storage; when differences appeared, frozen beat fresh-stored more often than the reverse (UGA/Pegg: vitamin content of refrigerated fresh produce fell vs fresh in every case; differences "not of huge magnitude"). |
| 5 | Worse than fresh ones? Myth! | TRUE | Li et al. 2017 conclusion above; Favell 1998 (Food Chemistry 62:59–64): frozen peas and broccoli similar to typical shop-bought fresh and better than peas kept at home several days; frozen spinach clearly better than all market spinach. |
| 6 | Fresh looks better, but its vitamins fade after picking | TRUE | Rickman, Barrett & Bruhn 2007 (J Sci Food Agric 87:930–944, UC Davis review): vitamin C "begins to degrade ... immediately after harvest" and "degrades steadily during prolonged storage". |

Caveats in the description (plain words): blanching before freezing costs some vitamin C, and frozen vegetables also lose
some slowly over months in the freezer; just-picked fresh produce is best of all; check bags for added salt or sauces;
don't overboil either kind. Minerals and fibre are not changed much by freezing.

Sources
- Li L, Pegg RB, Eitenmiller RR, Chun JY, Kerrihard AL (2017). Selected nutrient analyses of fresh, fresh-stored, and frozen fruits and vegetables. J Food Compos Anal. doi:10.1016/j.jfca.2017.02.002 — https://digitalcommons.montclair.edu/nutr-foodstudies-facpubs/93
- Teachers College, Columbia University, Tisch Center: "Fresh, refrigerated, or frozen: does it really matter?" — https://www.tc.columbia.edu/tisch/blog/news/2023/fresh-refrigerated-or-frozen-does-it-really-matter-/
- Rickman JC, Barrett DM, Bruhn CM (2007). Nutritional comparison of fresh, frozen and canned fruits and vegetables. Part 1. J Sci Food Agric 87:930–944 — https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2010-06/19187.pdf
- Favell DJ (1998). A comparison of the vitamin C content of fresh and frozen vegetables. Food Chemistry 62(1):59–64.
