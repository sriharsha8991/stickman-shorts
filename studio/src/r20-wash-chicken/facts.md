# R20 · Does washing raw chicken make it safer? — facts (verified 2026-11-14)

Verdict: **MYTH** — rinsing raw chicken does not make it safe; it spreads germs around the kitchen. Cooking kills them.
Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source (read on the day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | In a US test, six in ten washers got germs in the sink | TRUE | USDA press release, 20 Aug 2019 (observational study): "Of the participants who washed their raw poultry, 60% had bacteria in their sink after washing or rinsing the poultry." https://www.usda.gov/node/30867 |
| 3 | Some stayed even after cleaning | TRUE | Same release: "14% of participants still had bacteria in their sinks after they attempted to clean the sink." |
| 4 | Salt or lemon won't kill them | TRUE | USDA release (https://www.usda.gov/node/30846): "Washing, rinsing, or brining meat and poultry in salt water, vinegar or lemon juice does not destroy bacteria." |
| 5 | Washing for safety? A myth! | TRUE | USDA FSIS "Washing Food: Does it Promote Food Safety?": washing raw poultry "is not recommended"; it "increases the risk for cross-contamination in the kitchen." https://www.fsis.usda.gov/es/node/3297 |
| 6 | Only cooking kills them. Wash your hands instead. | TRUE | FSIS: cooking "kills germs on meat and poultry"; poultry is safe at 165 °F (74 °C), checked with a food thermometer. USDA 2019: 31% of NON-washers still got bacteria onto salad lettuce, likely from poor handwashing and contaminated sinks/utensils — so hands and surfaces matter. |

Caveats said in plain words / in the description:
- The USDA test measured bacteria in sinks and on salad lettuce; it did not count people getting ill.
- Washers (26%) and non-washers (31%) both moved bacteria onto lettuce: the script does NOT claim washers contaminated salads more. The point is the sink plus hands.
- "US test" = USDA observational study in test kitchens with home cooks (2019). Exact participant count not stated in the release.
