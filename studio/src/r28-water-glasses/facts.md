# R28 · Do you really need eight glasses of water a day? — facts (verified 16 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **MYTH** — there is no fixed "eight glasses for everyone" rule. Water needs vary a lot from person to person, food
supplies part of your water, and for healthy people thirst is a good guide. Heat, exercise and some life stages raise needs.
Education, not medical advice (people with kidney, heart or other conditions, or with a doctor's fluid advice, follow that).

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: "Are you wasting effort on eight glasses before you're thirsty?" | question | — |
| 2 | One study tracked over five thousand people | TRUE | Yamada, Zhang, Henderson … Speakman et al. (2022), "Variation in human water turnover associated with environmental and lifestyle factors", *Science* 378(6622):909, doi:10.1126/science.abm8668 — 5,604 people aged 8 days to 96 years, measured with doubly labelled water (Univ. of Aberdeen release, 24 Nov 2022; ScienceDaily 25 Nov 2022). |
| 3 | Heat and exercise changed their needs | TRUE | Same study: energy expenditure (activity) was the biggest factor; water turnover was higher in hot, humid places, at altitude, in athletes and very active people (Aberdeen release). |
| 4 | A fifth of your water comes from food | TRUE (average) | National Academies, Dietary Reference Intakes for water (report release, 11 Feb 2004): about 80% of total water from drinks and "the other 20 percent is derived from food". (Speakman estimates food can supply even more for many people.) |
| 5 | Eight glasses for everyone? Myth! | TRUE | National Academies: "We don't offer any rule of thumb based on how many glasses of water people should drink each day." Speakman (Aberdeen release): "a 'one size fits all policy' for water intake is not supported by these data"; the 8-glass / ~2 L advice is "probably too high for most people in most situations". |
| 6 | Hot day or workout? Drink more, and follow your thirst | TRUE | National Academies: healthy people "adequately meet their daily hydration needs by letting thirst be their guide"; heat and exercise "may raise daily fluid needs"; very active people in hot weather can need 6 L or more of total water. |

Caveats in the description: thirst is a guide for healthy people; drink more in heat, exercise, pregnancy or breastfeeding;
older adults and people with medical conditions or a doctor's fluid advice should follow that advice. Not a reason to drink less
than you need.
