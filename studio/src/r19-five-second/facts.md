# R19 · Is food safe on the floor for five seconds? — facts (verified 2026-11-13)

Verdict: **MYTH** (bacteria can move onto dropped food in under one second; the "five-second rule" is not a safe window).

Primary source, read on the day: Robyn C. Miranda & Donald W. Schaffner, "Longer Contact Times Increase Cross-Contamination
of *Enterobacter aerogenes* from Surfaces to Food", *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* (2016), doi:10.1128/AEM.01838-16
(Rutgers University). Accepted manuscript: https://nature.berkeley.edu/garbelottoat/wp-content/uploads/miranda-and-schaffner-2016.pdf
Rutgers press release via ScienceDaily: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/09/160909112406.htm

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | "Scientists ran over two thousand drop tests." | True | 4 surfaces (stainless steel, ceramic tile, wood, carpet) × 4 foods (watermelon, white bread, buttered bread, gummy candy) × 4 contact times (<1, 5, 30, 300 s) × 2 inocula = 128 scenarios, each repeated 20 times = 2,560 measurements. Card says "2,560 drop tests · Rutgers, 2016". |
| 3 | "Germs moved onto food in under one second." | True | Paper: "Some transfer takes place 'instantaneously' at times <1 s". E.g. watermelon from stainless steel at <1 s ≈ 91% transfer (buffer inoculum). |
| 4 | "Wet watermelon got the most germs." | True | Watermelon had the highest transfer (about 0.2–97% across conditions); gummy candy the lowest. The authors name moisture as a key driver. |
| 5 | "The five-second rule? A myth!" | True | Authors: "The 5-second rule is a significant oversimplification of what actually happens." |
| 6 | "Carpet passed the fewest germs, but longer meant more." | True | Carpet transferred far less than tile or steel (authors: "lower transfer rates (~0.2%) when transfer was from carpet to food"); contact time had a highly significant effect (P<0.000001): longer contact transferred more. |

Caveats (plain words, in the description): one lab test with one bacterium (*Enterobacter aerogenes*, used as a stand-in for Salmonella) put on
clean lab surfaces at high levels; real floors vary. Less germs is not the same as safe. Education, not medical advice.
