# T31 facts (checked 2026-11-04)
| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You hear your own voice through air and through your bones (the head). | Confirmed. Sound reaches the inner ear by air and by bone conduction; the bone part dominated between 1 and 2 kHz for most phonemes tested. | Reinfeldt, Östli, Håkansson & Stenfelt 2010, J Acoust Soc Am 128(2), abstract via Chalmers: https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/125312 |
| A recording has only the air part. | Confirmed as physics of a microphone in air: it picks up airborne sound, not your skull's vibration. Abstract above measures air conduction at the open ear canal. Plain-language support: Time 2017 and Guardian 2018 as summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_confrontation (secondary). | as above |
| Other people hear the voice on the recording. | Adjusted. Others hear your voice by air only, so the recording is close, not identical. On screen: "Close to it: a phone microphone changes the sound a little." | Same |
| This is why the voice feels wrong. | Kept with a caveat. Part of the reason: expectations and self-image also play a role. On screen: "One main reason, not the only one." Not said: "deeper" (row caveat). | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_confrontation (secondary, cites Time 2017, Guardian 2018) |
| Everyone hates it. | Not claimed. Only asked as a question. People shown recordings without knowing rated their own voices more attractive than outsiders did (Hughes & Harrison 2013), so a blanket claim would be false. | https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/i-like-my-voice-better-self-enhancement-bias-in-perceptions-of-vo/ |
