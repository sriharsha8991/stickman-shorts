# R36 · Brushed your teeth? Before you rinse, does water ruin it? — facts (verified 19 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **HALF TRUE**. Rinsing with a lot of water after brushing lowers the fluoride left in the mouth (lab/saliva
studies), but a three-year school trial found no clear difference in new decay between children who rinsed with water and
children who only spat. The NHS still advises: spit, don't rinse. Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | In 2001, big rinses left less fluoride. | True | Sjögren K, Melin NH. "The influence of rinsing routines on fluoride retention after toothbrushing." Gerodontology 2001;18(1):15-20 (PMID 11813383). Randomised cross-over, 21 dental students; "Rinsing with a higher volume of water substantially decreased fluoride retention in all samples" (saliva, interdental fluid, plaque); rinse duration made no difference. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11813383/ |
| 3 | A three-year study compared rinsers and spitters. | True | Machiulskiene V, Richards A, Nyvad B, Baelum V. "Prospective study of the effect of post-brushing rinsing behaviour on dental caries." Caries Research 2002 (PMID 12399689). 407 children (mean 11.8 y) in three schools in Kaunas, Lithuania; 276 examined at 3 years. School A brushed with fluoride toothpaste and rinsed thoroughly with water; school B spat only; school C control (no supervised brushing). https://read.qxmd.com/read/12399689 |
| 4 | Their cavities were nearly equal. (card: RINSED 6.8 / SPAT ONLY 6.2) | True | Same study: 3-year DMFS increments (decayed, missing, filled surfaces incl. non-cavitated lesions) 6.8 (95% CI 5.3-8.3) rinsed vs 6.2 (4.6-7.8) spat only, not significantly different; both far below the control school 12.4 (10.6-14.1), p < 0.001. |
| 5 | Rinsing ruins it? Half true! | Half true | Lines 2-4: fluoride retention drops with big rinses, but the cavity difference in the trial was small and not significant. Other work is mixed (Chestnutt et al. 1998, Community Dent Oral Epidemiol, PMID 9870540: 2,621 adolescents, caries increment "significantly related" to self-reported rinsing method; not used on screen because the abstract does not give the direction). |
| 6 | Fluoride still drops, so the NHS says spit, don't rinse. | True | NHS "How to keep your teeth clean": "Don't rinse your mouth immediately after brushing", rinsing washes away the concentrated fluoride; adults use toothpaste with at least 1,350 ppm fluoride, brush twice a day for about 2 minutes. https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/ |

Plain-words caveat: the trial was in children in one city and the effect of rinsing was small; skipping the rinse costs
nothing, so the NHS advice still stands. Education, not medical advice.
