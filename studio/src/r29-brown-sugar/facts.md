# R29 · Is brown sugar healthier than white sugar? — facts (verified 17 Nov 2026)

Verdict: **MYTH** — brown sugar is not meaningfully healthier than white sugar. Calories are almost the same and the extra
minerals from molasses are tiny. Both count as added (free) sugar. Education, not medical advice.

| Line | Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: "Brown sugar? Before you pay more, is it healthier?" | question | — |
| 2 | It is often white sugar plus molasses | TRUE | EUFIC (updated 26 Mar 2026): brown sugar "is either less refined so that some molasses remain" or "is made from refined white sugar with a small amount of molasses added back during processing". |
| 3 | It has two percent fewer calories | TRUE (rounded) | USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy: "Sugars, brown" 380 kcal/100 g vs "Sugars, granulated" (FDC 169655) 387 kcal/100 g → 1.8% fewer (EUFIC table: 190 vs 194 kcal per 50 g). |
| 4 | A teaspoon gives under one percent of daily calcium | TRUE | USDA SR Legacy brown sugar 83 mg calcium/100 g (EUFIC: 41.5 mg per 50 g) → ~3.5 mg per 4.2 g teaspoon; even the Norwegian Food Composition Table's higher value (185 mg/100 g, Mattilsynet) gives ~8 mg. NIH ODS: adults 19–50 need 1,000 mg/day → 0.3–0.8%. |
| 5 | Healthier than white sugar? Myth! | TRUE | EUFIC: "nutritional profile and calorie content are nearly identical"; mineral amounts "extremely small and do not make a meaningful nutritional contribution". |
| 6 | It looks natural, but your body counts both as sugar | TRUE | Both are almost entirely sucrose (USDA: brown ~97 g, granulated ~100 g carbohydrate per 100 g); EUFIC/EFSA treat both as added sugars. |

Caveats in the description: brown sugar tastes different (molasses) and that is a fine reason to choose it; unrefined
sugars such as jaggery vary by product but are still mostly sugar. Not about artificial sweeteners.

Sources
- EUFIC, "Is brown sugar healthier than white sugar?" (updated 26 Mar 2026): https://www.eufic.org/en/misinformation/article/is-brown-sugar-healthier-than-white-sugar (cites USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy "Sugars, granulated" and "Sugars, brown")
- USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy, "Sugars, granulated" FDC 169655 (387 kcal, 1 mg calcium per 100 g)
