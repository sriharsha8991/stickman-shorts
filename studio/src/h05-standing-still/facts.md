# H05 · Does your standing desk protect your heart? — facts (checked 2026-11-01)

Hack row H05 ("Your standing desk isn't the fix", Move, Myth vs Reality, Health-edu). Row facts re-verified on the day;
the newer 2024 cohort study replaces the row's "standing still is still still" twist (no wordplay under the 2026-10-29
clarity rules) with a sourced reveal.

| Claim in the Short | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "The desk helps you sit less." Sit-stand desks reduce sitting at work. | confirmed (very low-certainty evidence) | Cochrane review update, Shrestha et al. 2018 (CD010912.pub4): 34 studies, 3,397 people; sit-stand desks cut sitting by 84–116 min per workday; evidence very low quality; health benefits unproven. https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/featured-review-workplace-interventions-reducing-time-spent-sitting-work ; the row's 2016 version (20 studies, 2,174 people, 30 min–2 h): https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/health-effects-sit-stand-desks-and-interventions-aimed-reduce-sitting-work-are-still-unproven |
| "Your legs stay still, like a statue." Standing is still stationary; it barely raises energy use. | confirmed (framing) | Cochrane 2018 news item: "standing barely increases energy expenditure". The 2024 study calls sitting + standing "stationary behaviour". |
| "Scientists tracked eighty-three thousand people for years." "A wrist tracker measured how long they stood." | confirmed | Ahmadi M. et al., "Device-measured stationary behaviour and cardiovascular and orthostatic circulatory disease incidence", Int J Epidemiol 2024, doi:10.1093/ije/dyae136: 83,013 UK Biobank adults free of heart disease, research-grade wrist-worn trackers, followed ~7–8 years. University of Sydney release, 16 Oct 2024: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1061454 |
| "People who stood more did not have healthier hearts." | confirmed (observational: "not linked to" lower risk) | Same study/release: "standing more over the long term did not improve cardiovascular health" (coronary heart disease, stroke, heart failure). On screen: "NOT HEALTHIER" + source line "observational · all standing, not just desks". |
| "Your heart wants you to move, not just stand." / "So let your desk remind you to walk." | the researchers' advice, not a measured result | Same release: "people who sit for long periods should build in regular movement throughout the day" (breaks, walking around, walking meetings, stairs). Stamatakis: standing for too long will not offset a sedentary lifestyle. |

Kept out (one idea, max two numbers): sitting > 10 h/day linked to higher heart + circulatory risk; standing more linked
to more circulatory problems (varicose veins) in the 2024 study; the 84–116 minutes; the team's earlier 6-minute
vigorous exercise finding. Caveats on screen: "Education, not medical advice"; source line says the 2024 study is
observational and measured all standing (the release notes desks are a very small part of it), and the desk review's
evidence is weak.
