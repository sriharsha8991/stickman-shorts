| "Scientists tested this with headphones. Each ear heard a different voice. People repeated one voice and ignored the other." | Confirmed. Dichotic listening with shadowing: participants repeat (shadow) the message in one ear and ignore the other ear (Cherry 1953 method; used by Moray 1959 and Wood & Cowan 1995). | Conway, Cowan & Bunting 2001, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 8(2):331–335 (method + review): https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/conway-et-al-pbr-2001.pdf |
| "Then the ignored voice said their own name." | Confirmed. The participant's own name was inserted into the ignored channel. | Moray 1959, QJEP 11:56–60; Wood & Cowan 1995, JEP:LMC 21(1):255–260 |
| "About one in three people noticed it." | Confirmed. Moray 1959: about 33% reported hearing their own name; Wood & Cowan 1995: 34.6%. | Conway et al. 2001 (cites both); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Moray |
| "That's because your brain still checks ignored sounds." | Confirmed as the standard reading (simplified): Moray concluded almost nothing from the rejected ear gets through except subjectively important messages such as your own name, so the ignored channel is still processed to some degree.
